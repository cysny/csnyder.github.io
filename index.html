Case study 1 · Patient experience

Healthcare systems assumed a single race. Multiracial patients felt it.

Racialized experiences in care settings affect whether patients use care, follow treatment and trust providers. Multiracial people and families show those dynamics clearly, yet they're rarely studied.

The goal: identify how racial microaggressions show up in healthcare for multiracial people and families, and what would make care more inclusive.

My role
Lead researcher: research plan, interviews, analysis, findings and recommendations
Partners
Stakeholder advisory committee; project manager and qualitative researcher
Participants
31 people who identify as multiracial with a healthcare visit in the past 12 months
Methods
15 moderated interviews (remote and in person); 3 asynchronous online focus groups (16 people, new prompt every 7 days)

Collect

Met people where they were: in-person interviews at locations participants chose, Zoom interviews, and an early online focus group platform.

Analyze

Coded open-ended responses and built themes across interviews and focus groups.

Share

Briefed care teams and stakeholders. Published 2 articles and gave 2 conference talks plus internal seminars.

What we learned

Mistaken assumptions

Providers and staff guessed wrong about family relationships and racial identity.

Overlapping identities

Race combined with gender, income and ability to shape each experience.

Persistent stereotypes

Participants met stereotypes about being multiracial and about people of color.

Structural bias

Everyday systems like intake forms and family imagery assumed one race.

Impact

  • Recommendations to make patient-provider interactions more responsive to multiracial patients and people of color.
  • Findings set the priorities for follow-on studies of multiracial patient experience and health-seeking behavior.

What I'd carry forward

  • Asynchronous focus groups boosted participation and surfaced richer peer insight. That shaped how we designed later studies.

Small design choices, like a form field, can signal who a system was built for.

Case study 2 · Employee experience

Managers valued the resources. They were drowning in the volume.

A learning and development team needed to reach a large, diverse manager community with updates and growth opportunities. Managers were flooded with information and struggled to spot what needed action.

The goal: learn how well L&D communications met manager needs, and find ways to strengthen manager learning.

My role
Lead researcher: defined program goals with leads; designed, ran, analyzed and reported the survey
Partners
Manager learning lead; communications team liaison
Participants
People managers across the organization
Methods
Key informant interviews to define outcomes; organization-wide online survey that also set a baseline for ongoing monitoring

Collect

Interviewed the manager learning and communications leads, then surveyed all people managers.

Analyze

Paired descriptive statistics with coded open-ended responses to explain the numbers.

Share

Presented the report and recommendations to L&D leadership.

What we learned

Information overload

Managers valued the resources but felt overwhelmed by how much arrived.

What landed

Policies, professional development, rewards, training and benefits updates worked best.

One central hub

Managers wanted one home for resources, plus shorter, more targeted messages.

Impact

  • The learning team adapted its communication approach to real manager needs.
  • Identified clear chances to streamline messaging and centralize manager resources.

What happened next

  • Set the direction for continued work on manager learning and resource development.

More content isn't more value. Making things easy to find is what helps.

Case study 3 · Workforce experience

Racism shaped how Black nurses coped, and how far their careers could go.

Long-term care struggles to keep nursing professionals. Racialized experiences affect nurses of color, and through them, the care, the patient experience and turnover.

The goal: understand how Black nursing professionals in long-term care experience and cope with racism, and identify interventions that support their wellbeing and advancement.

My role
Lead researcher: research plan, interviews, analysis, findings and recommendations
Partners
Stakeholder advisory committee; project manager and qualitative researcher
Participants
18 adult nursing professionals in long-term care who identify as Black or African American
Methods
Moderated interviews, remote and in person

Collect

Interviews in person at locations participants chose, or by phone or Zoom around demanding shift schedules.

Analyze

Coded interviews and built themes on experiences, coping and career paths.

Share

Shared recommendations with employers and training institutions. Published 1 article and gave 2 conference talks plus internal seminars.

What we learned

Racialized experiences

Nurses faced racism from patients and colleagues, from individuals and from the system itself.

Coping strategies

They coped through journaling, social support, high performance and advocacy.

Career development

These experiences shaped career paths and limited chances to advance.

Impact

  • Recommendations to employers and training institutions to support the growth and retention of nursing professionals.

What I'd carry forward

  • Recruit on participants' terms: meet when and where it's convenient.
  • Partner with community and professional organizations to build access and trust.

Retention problems are often experience problems. Listening closely shows where to step in.
